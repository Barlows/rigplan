What it does

A schematic tool that knows the electrical rules.

Most drawing tools stop at the picture. RigPlan keeps the arithmetic in step with the drawing, so a change in cable length or charger rating flows straight through to the schedule.

Cable sizing as you draw

Current follows the diagram from each load back to its source. RigPlan picks the smallest cable that passes both ampacity and voltage drop, then shows the working.

Fuses coordinated with cable

A fuse is only useful if it is smaller than the cable's derated ampacity and larger than the continuous load. Every run is checked against both limits.

Safety checks that explain themselves

Missing battery fuse, unprotected inverter feed, shore power without an RCD, mixed chemistries, undersized PV cable — each finding says what to fix and why.

Schedules and a real BOM

Component list, cable schedule and fuse schedule are built from the drawing. Export them as CSV, or take the diagram out as SVG, PNG or PDF.

Three standards, one switch

ABYC E-11 for boats and RVs, BS 7671 / IEC 60364 for European builds, and NEC for US installations. Ampacity, derating and voltage-drop targets change together.

Yours, on your machine

Projects live in your browser's local storage. No sign-up, no seat count, no upload. Named versions let you keep a revision before a big change.

The drawing

Place parts. Run cables. Read the result.

Drag a component from the library onto the sheet, then click one terminal and another to run a cable. Re-route by dragging; the cable follows the terminals and keeps its label with it.

Reference data

Published tables, not guessed numbers.

Ampacity comes from the standard's own table. Ambient temperature, grouping and installation method are applied as separate factors, so the result can be audited line by line.

Boats & RVs

ABYC E-11

Table VI-A ampacity, Table VI-B ambient correction, 105 °C boat cable by default, 3% or 10% voltage-drop targets.

Europe

BS 7671 / IEC 60364

Table 4D1A with Methods A1, B1, C and D, Table 4B1 ambient and Table 4C1 grouping factors.

North America

NEC (NFPA 70)

Table 310.16 ampacity at 60, 75 and 90 °C, ambient correction and conductor bundling adjustment.

RigPlan is a planning aid. It does not certify an installation. Have the finished design checked by a qualified marine or electrical engineer, and follow the manufacturer's instructions for every part.

Questions

Before you start cutting cable.

Is RigPlan a CAD program?

No. It is a single-line wiring planner. It draws components as symbols, treats cables as connections with a length and current, and focuses on the electrical decisions rather than mechanical layout or enclosure design.

Where are my projects stored?

In your browser's local storage on this device. There is no account and no server copy. Use Project → Export JSON for a backup, and Import to move a project to another machine.

Can I override the suggested cable or fuse?

Yes. Select a run and choose a cable size or fuse rating from the inspector. The safety checks still run, and the result panel tells you whether the manual choice fails ampacity, voltage drop or fuse coordination.

Does it cover AC as well as DC?

Yes. Shore inlets, RCBOs, AC panels, sockets, induction hobs and inverter outputs use AC conductors with their own colours and checks, including the AC/DC separation rule and shore-power RCD check.

Is the result guaranteed to pass inspection?

No, and no calculator can promise that. The engine implements published ampacity and voltage-drop rules conservatively, but installation details, local amendments, manufacturer instructions and a qualified person's judgement still decide. Use it to get close, then have the design reviewed.
